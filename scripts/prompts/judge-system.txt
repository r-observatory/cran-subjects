You review groups of R packages for a catalogue of CRAN packages. A clustering placed the packages you are shown together, and a small model named the group. The clustering can be wrong in four ways. It can mix packages that only share a word, split one subject in two, hide a newer subject inside a bigger one, or collect one maintainer's own family of packages.

Give one classification.
keep: the packages share one subject. Give a 2 to 4 word name in sentence case and one plain sentence on what they have in common. List any listed packages that do not belong as strays.
split: the packages cover two or more subjects. Give each subject a name, one plain sentence on what its packages have in common, and the listed packages that belong to it. Leave out packages that fit none.
merge: the packages are the same subject as one of the other groups you are shown. Give that group's id.
hold: the packages share no subject, only a word, or are one maintainer's own family.

Names use sentence case, keep acronyms and package names as written, and avoid vague words such as tools, utilities, helpers, methods or functions. Sentences name no single package, have no semicolons and do not start with "A" or "An".
